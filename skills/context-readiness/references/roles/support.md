# Role: Customer Support / Success

Give a frustrated customer a correct answer quickly, know what has already been
promised to them, and never contradict what a colleague said last week.

## Six dimensions

1. **Escalation** — a named path, the people on it, committed response times.
2. **Known issues** — current issues and workarounds, tracked and dated. A
   tracker last touched months ago caps this at 2.
3. **Account history** — per customer: what they use, what went wrong, what
   was promised. Promises recorded nowhere cap this at 1.
4. **Approved answers** — the questions customers ask most, with one approved
   answer each. Two different answers to the same question cap this at 2.
5. **Commitments** — contractual SLAs and refund/cancellation policy, and
   where actual performance against them is recorded.
6. **Feeds** — tickets, calls and chat connected with real data. Scored from
   the map.

## The draft — a reply to an escalated customer

Six slots: acknowledgement of their specific history · what went wrong ·
what we committed to · the workaround or fix · the timeline · who owns it.

## The ten questions

1. What is the escalation path, and who is on it? `[escalation]`
2. What response times have we committed to? `[escalation]`
3. What known issues might a customer hit this week? `[known-issues]`
4. Pick a named customer: what have we promised them that is still open?
   `[account-history]`
5. What are the five most-asked questions and our approved answers? `[faq]`
6. What do we contractually commit to on uptime and support? `[sla]`
7. Are we currently meeting those commitments, and where does it say so? `[sla]`
8. What is the refund and cancellation policy, and who approves exceptions?
   `[refunds]`
9. Which metrics go into a quarterly review, and where do they come from? `[qbr]`
10. What have customers asked for that we said is coming? `[roadmap-asks]`

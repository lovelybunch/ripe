# Role: SDR (Sales Development)

The job, in context terms: describe what you sell accurately in one breath, know who to say it to, produce outbound materials that don't come back for a rewrite, and answer inbound questions fast and correctly.

## The six dimensions

1. **Products & pricing** — every offering named and described; pricing posture stated. ("Custom — talk to us" counts as an answer. Silence, or two sources disagreeing, does not.)
2. **Customers & proof** — who you sell to and who you don't; quotes, logos, and examples with permission noted per item.
3. **Positioning & competitors** — one current version of the pitch; named competitors with when-you-win and when-you-lose.
4. **Materials** — live test 1, below.
5. **Answers** — live test 2, below.
6. **Plumbing & voice** — CRM, call notes, and documents connected *with real data in them*; an email voice and templates that sound like you.

Scoring notes: score the data, not the connection — a live CRM full of stale records is a 2. Conflicting pricing caps dimension 1 at 2. Customer quotes without permission status cap dimension 2 at 2.

## Live test 1 — the one-pager

Using **only** what the map found (no outside knowledge, no guessing), draft a one-pager skeleton — six slots: headline, problem, solution, two proof points, pricing-or-CTA line. Mark every slot **[cited: source]** or **[INVENTED]**. A plausible guess is still invented — plausible inventions are the dangerous ones.

Show the annotated skeleton in the report and headline the **invented count out of 6**. Score: 4 = zero invented · 3 = one · 2 = two · 1 = three or more · 0 = couldn't attempt at all.

## Live test 2 — ten questions

Answer each from found context only. Verdicts: **cited** / **uncertain** / **can't answer**.
The ids in brackets match `roles/sdr.md` in `ripe`, so a map and a measurement line up.

1. What does it do, in two sentences? `[what-it-does]`
2. Who is it for — and who is it *not* for? `[icp]`
3. What does it cost? `[pricing]`
4. How is it different from [your main competitor]? `[competitor]`
5. Where does our data live, and who can see it? `[data-residency]`
6. How long does it take to get started? `[getting-started]`
7. What does it integrate with? `[integrations]`
8. Do you have a customer like us? `[proof]`
9. What support do we get? `[support]`
10. Can we talk to a reference? `[reference]`

Headline the **cited count out of 10**. Score: 4 = 9–10 · 3 = 7–8 · 2 = 4–6 · 1 = 1–3 · 0 = none. Every "can't answer" becomes a plan item.

## The starter page set (for the plan)

1. One page per product or service — what it is, who it's for, why you, pricing posture.
2. Who we sell to — including who to walk away from.
3. The pitch — the current version, plus claims you've retired.
4. Q&A library — seeded from test 2's misses, grown from real inbound questions.
5. Proof — every quote and logo with its permission status.
6. Competitor one-liners — a sentence each on when you win and lose; grow into battlecards later.

Then the feeds that keep it alive: call notes → the Q&A library and customer language; CRM → account pages; wins and losses → the proof page. This step is what separates a context layer from a folder of documents — don't let a plan end without it.

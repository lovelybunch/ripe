# Role: Marketer

Say what sales says, in the company's voice, to the people the company has
actually chosen to sell to — and know what worked last time.

## Six dimensions

1. **Positioning & claims** — one current pitch; retired claims marked as
   retired. Two live versions of the pitch cap this at 2.
2. **Audience** — named segments with a person and a pain in each.
3. **Voice** — written tone rules and words we avoid. Voice that exists only
   in old copy caps this at 2.
4. **Proof** — quotes, logos and case studies with permission status per
   item. Missing permission status caps this at 2.
5. **Competitors** — a one-line differentiator per named competitor, dated.
6. **Performance** — campaign and channel results recorded somewhere, with
   dates. Scored from the map: results that live only in a dashboard nobody
   exported count as fragments.

## The draft — a campaign brief

Six slots: audience · the one claim · proof point · competitor contrast ·
call to action · the channel and why.

## The ten questions

1. What is our current positioning statement, in one sentence? `[positioning]`
2. Which claims have we retired, and why? `[positioning]`
3. Who exactly are we talking to, and what do they care about? `[audience]`
4. What are the voice rules a writer must follow? `[voice]`
5. Which words or phrases do we avoid? `[voice]`
6. Which competitors do we position against, and with what line? `[competitors]`
7. What ran last quarter and what were the results? `[campaigns]`
8. Which channel is performing best right now? `[channels]`
9. Which customer quotes and logos are cleared for public use? `[proof]`
10. What is on the calendar for the next month, and who owns it? `[calendar]`
